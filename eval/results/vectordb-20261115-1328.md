## What it shows

- The search graph (HNSW) loses nothing at this size: exact search scores the same (63.5) and
  is slower. A bigger graph adds nothing. The defaults stay.
- Compressing the vectors costs no accuracy here: int8 (4x smaller) and binary (32x smaller)
  both score 63.4, since the top candidates are re-scored with the full vectors. Keeping the
  full vectors on disk and only int8 copies in RAM cuts the vectors' RAM to a quarter
  (159 -> 40 MB here), same accuracy and speed. Chosen: matters on Qdrant Cloud's small plans.
- Merging the rankings by score distribution (DBSF) is slightly worse than reciprocal rank
  fusion (63.1 vs 63.5): RRF stays.
- BM25 word weights help most: alone instead of bge-m3's word weights 65.2; together with them
  65.7. Before the reranker the gain is larger (56.3 -> 61.5), and the right document is among
  the reranker's 30 candidates more often (82.8% -> 86.9%). Chosen.
- The chosen setting, run through the pipeline's own code: 65.6 nDCG@5 (was 63.5), right
  document first 54.3% (was 52.9%), 40 MB of vectors in RAM (was 159), 10 ms per search.

Server memory (resident) barely moves: at 40,000 chunks it is mostly payload (the chunk text),
keyword indexes and Qdrant itself, not the dense vectors. The vectors' share is what grows with
the collection, and what compression and disk storage reduce.

Numbers are lower than the 69.9 measured without decoys (tatdqa-vidore-20261028-1626): here
FinQA's 2,789 report pages compete with the right ones.
